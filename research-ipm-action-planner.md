# IPM Action Planner — research basis

## Sources and implementation findings

### U.S. Environmental Protection Agency: IPM Principles
Source: https://www.epa.gov/safepestcontrol/integrated-pest-management-ipm-principles

The EPA describes IPM as an economically and environmentally sensitive approach that combines pest life-cycle information with available control methods. Its four-tiered approach is: set action thresholds; monitor and identify pests; prevent pest pressure; and select control methods, using less risky and targeted options before resorting to broad pesticide applications. The planner therefore treats scouting and threshold evidence as prerequisites for a chemical recommendation and surfaces cultural, mechanical, biological, and targeted chemical options in that order.

### UC IPM: Establishing Action Thresholds
Source: https://ipm.ucanr.edu/agriculture/floriculture-and-ornamental-nurseries/establishing-treatment-thresholds/

UC IPM defines an action threshold as the pest abundance or damage level beyond which management action should be taken. Thresholds should be quantitative, developed through consistent monitoring and records, and adapted to crop stage, crop value, time to harvest, market conditions, and the effectiveness and risk of available controls. The planner therefore distinguishes monitoring from action and records a quantitative average observation against a configurable threshold.

### UC IPM: Managing Pesticide Resistance
Source: https://ipm.ucanr.edu/agriculture/floriculture-and-ornamental-nurseries/managing-pesticide-resistance/

UC IPM recommends using pesticides only when monitoring and thresholds justify them, avoiding more than two consecutive applications with the same mode of action, rotating between different modes of action, and minimizing unnecessary or persistent applications. The planner therefore includes mode-of-action history, a same-MOA consecutive-application warning, and a rotation prompt rather than recommending a product or rate.
